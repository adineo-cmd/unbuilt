About the census

What this is, how it was put together, and where it will be wrong.

What this is

Unbuilt is a census of human making. It lists ideas in twelve fields and splits each field three ways: things the world already has plenty of, things that exist but were never finished properly, and things it is still missing. The premise is simple — in every field, most people build the same ten things, because those ten things are visible. The interesting work hides in the outlined and dashed cards, which are invisible precisely because nobody has made them yet — or nobody has made them whole.

What the three columns mean

Already built means the shelf is full: mature products, many funded competitors, no unsolved problem left worth your years. The crowdedness marks are judgement, not measurement — five squares means you would be entering the most crowded room in the field.

Half-built means it exists, but not properly: too expensive, locked to one country or vendor, painful to use, or stuck at pilot scale. These are often the best openings on the census — the riskiest question, "does anyone actually want this?", is already answered by whatever exists, and the assignment is to finish what stalled. Each card says what is missing and why it stalled.

Not built yet means not built well enough, cheaply enough, or available where most people live. It is not a claim that nobody ever tried. Many of these gaps are open for good reasons — regulation, thin margins, missing data — and the page says which reason blocks each one. A gap with a blocker is an assignment, not a warning.

How it was compiled

The census was written in October 2026, combining public reporting with judgement. Saturation claims were cross-checked against 2026 sources, including preuve.ai's ranked list of AI startup ideas, a long Hacker News debate on why founders avoid novel problems, and the Lemelson Foundation's work on hardware invention for underserved markets. Everything else — the crowdedness marks, the difficulty marks, the "closest attempt" attributions — is editorial opinion and should be treated as such.

  • preuve.ai — AI startup ideas 2026, ranked
  • Hacker News — why most startups don't solve novel problems
  • Lemelson Foundation — hardware invention for global development

The shelves, and where they come from

The Shelves page backs "already built" with real catalogs rather than memory: Flathub and F-Droid in full from their open metadata dumps, the Apple App Store through its official Search API, popular npm packages through the registry API, and Google Play through capped, popularity-ranked searches{play_note}. The raw snapshots live in data/raw/, the full merged catalog in data/catalog.db, and the site ships a capped index at assets/catalog.json. Rerun pipeline/ingest.py and pipeline/build_site_index.py to refresh it — the census of ideas is editorial, but the shelves are measured.

Limits, stated plainly

This census goes stale the moment you read it. Someone may have started on a dashed card last week; two people may have finished one this morning. Before committing years to anything here, search for products, message the makers of the closest attempt, and assume the world moved since this was written. Being second with a real difference still beats being first into a crowd — but both beat building number four thousand and one.

Colophon

The whole site is static HTML generated by one Python script, set in Archivo and Source Serif 4, with no trackers and no cookies. The generator lives in build.py; edit the census data there and rebuild.
